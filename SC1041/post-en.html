Design details of the men's chino shorts

The shape is the classic one you find in any good menswear shop. The waist sits at the natural waistline, the seat is shaped with darts rather than a yoke, and the legs fall straight with enough room to sit and walk comfortably. Nothing is oversized and nothing is skin tight, which is exactly why the style works on so many body types.

Reading the sketch

On the front, the long diagonal line on each side is the slanted pocket opening; the shorter line next to it is a single waist dart. The curved line on the wearer's left is the fly topstitching, which hides the zip. The small rectangles on the waistband are the belt loops, one on each front.

On the back there are no pockets at all, only two darts on each side, the centre back seam and the remaining belt loops. Six loops in total hold a belt in place without twisting.

  • Slanted front pockets with lining bags that reach the fly, so the front stays flat
  • Zip fly with a fly shield, left over right in the menswear direction
  • One waist dart on each front, two on each back
  • Two-piece waistband, 4 cm (1 5/8″) finished, with a button and a trouser hook
  • Six belt loops and an above-the-knee length with a 2 cm hem

The zip fly is the only step that asks for real attention. The instructions break it into small moves, and once you have done one fly you will reuse the method on every pair of trousers you make afterwards.

Sizing guide

Measure the waist where you normally wear your trousers and the hip around the fullest part of the seat, then pick the size from the table. Common fit adjustments

Length. The pattern is drafted for 180 cm. If you are shorter or taller, or you simply like your shorts a little longer, add or remove length at the hem of the fronts and backs and redraw the side and inside leg lines. Full guide: how to lengthen or shorten a sewing pattern.

Waist between sizes. Cut the size that fits your hip, then try the shorts on before the waistband goes on. You can take in or let out up to about 1 cm on each side of the centre back seam and shorten the waistband by the same amount.

Choosing fabric for chino shorts

Chino shorts need a fabric that holds a crease and keeps its shape after a day of wear. A mid-weight cotton twill (around 200 to 280 g/m²) is the classic choice and the easiest to sew: it presses beautifully, it does not shift under the presser foot and it forgives small mistakes on the fly.

For very hot weather, linen or a linen-cotton blend feels cooler but creases more, so it gives a more relaxed look. Light denim works too if you like a casual pair. Avoid fabrics with a lot of stretch: the waistband and the fly are drafted for a stable woven. More tips in our guide on how to choose fabric for sewing patterns.

Sewing notes

Fit and style

Sewing steps

Seam allowance 1 cm (3/8″) unless stated otherwise. Finish the raw edges with an overlocker or a zigzag as you go, and press every seam before it is crossed by another one.

  1. Prepare the pieces. Transfer all the markings and clip the notches. Fuse interfacing to both waistband pieces and to the fly, plus a 2 cm strip along each slanted pocket edge and a 3 cm strip on the fly extension of the right front. Finish the side seams, inside leg seams and the front crotch curves.
  2. Darts. Sew one dart on each front and two on each back, from the waist to the point. Knot the threads at the point and press the darts toward the centre.
  3. Slanted front pockets. Sew the small pocket bag to the slanted edge of each front, understitch and turn it inside. Stitch the pocket facing onto the big pocket bag, then sew the two bags together around the curve. Baste the bag at the fly and across the waist so it stays flat.
  4. Zip fly. Shorten the zip, make the fly shield and stitch the zip to it. Edgestitch the right front close to the teeth, sew the fly to the left front, then sew the crotch seam below the fly. Attach the free zip tape to the fly, topstitch the curve 3 cm from the centre front and bar tack the bottom.
  5. Inside leg seams. Sew each front to its back along the inside leg and press open.
  6. Crotch seam. Slip one leg inside the other and sew the rest of the crotch from the fly to the centre back waist. Stitch the lower curve a second time and trim.
  7. Side seams. Sew the side seams from waist to hem, catching the edge of the pocket bags, and press open. Check that the pocket openings lie flat.
  8. Belt loops and waistband. Make the loop strip and cut it into 6 loops of 7 cm. Join the two waistband halves at the centre back, baste the loops at their marks, then sew the waistband on, close the ends and finish the inside by hand or by stitching in the ditch. Add the buttonhole, button and hook, and fold the loops up to stitch them at the top.
  9. Hem. Turn up 2 cm, press and stitch 1.5 cm from the fold, or use a blind hem for an invisible finish.
  10. Finishing. Remove the basting and press the shorts with a cloth. Press a crease down each leg if you like the sharper look.

Tips and common mistakes

Pocket openings that gape. The slanted edge is cut on the bias, so it stretches easily. Fuse the interfacing strip before you sew anything and do not pull the edge when you stitch the lining.

A fly that shows the zip. Keep the right front fold right next to the teeth and check the centre front notches line up before you topstitch. Basting the fly closed first helps a lot on a first try.

Pocket lining that peeks out. Trim the seam to 5 mm and understitch the lining before you turn it, then press it 1 to 2 mm toward the inside.

Belt loops that tear off. Stitch each loop twice at the top and at the bottom. The loops and the bottom of the fly take most of the strain when you wear a belt.

How to style men's chino shorts

Smart casual: a polo shirt, a woven belt and loafers, ideal for a summer lunch or the office on a hot Friday.

Holiday: a linen shirt with the sleeves rolled up and leather sandals or espadrilles.

Weekend: a plain tee and white sneakers. Sew a second pair in navy or stone and you have a full summer wardrobe.

Care

Close the zip and wash inside out at 30 to 40 °C with similar colours. Iron with a pressing cloth on the right side and steam the creases back in.

Questions

Is seam allowance included?

Yes: 1 cm (3/8″) on every edge, plus a 2 cm (3/4″) hem. Only the belt loop strip has no allowance.

Which paper sizes are included?

The free download has A4 and US Letter files, one file per size, so you only print the size you need.

Which size should I choose?

Choose by your hip and waist body measurements in the size chart. Between two sizes, follow the hip and adjust the waist at the centre back seam.

Is this pattern good for a beginner?

It is rated intermediate because of the zip fly and the slanted pockets. A confident beginner can do it by following the steps slowly and basting the fly first.

What is the best fabric for chino shorts?

A mid-weight cotton twill or chino is the classic choice. Linen, linen-cotton, light denim and cotton gabardine also work well. Avoid stretchy fabrics.

Do the shorts have back pockets?

No. The back is kept clean with two darts on each side. The pattern sheet shows a back pocket mark that is not used in this design.

How long is the zip?

Buy a 15 to 18 cm (6 to 7 in) trouser zip. You shorten it to the fly opening of your size during step 4.

Can I make them longer?

Yes. Add length at the hem of the fronts and backs and redraw the side and inside leg seams. You can turn them into Bermuda shorts this way.
